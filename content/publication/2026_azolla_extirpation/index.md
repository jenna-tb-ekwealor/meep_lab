abstract: 'Previous studies have shown that there may be more species of <i>Azolla</i> in California than currently recognized, including a recent introduction of the non-native <i>Azolla caroliniana</i>. To better assess the status and species diversity of <i>Azolla</i> in California, we sequenced four historical herbarium specimens, some over 100 years old, and increased our sampling of extant populations across seven counties. We found that many specimens from California thought to be <i>Azolla microphylla</i> are actually <i>Azolla filiculoides</i> or members of one of two non-native <i>Azolla caroliniana</i> clades. We also found that some specimens collected from California from the 1960s through the 1980s fall within an <i>Azolla microphylla–mexicana</i> clade. However, there is no evidence of this species from any recent collections even from counties where it was previously found, which points to a potential extirpation of this species from California.'
